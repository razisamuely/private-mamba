% ===================== ABSTRACT =====================
\section*{Abstract}

Constrained multi-agent reinforcement learning is usually solved reactively: a policy learns to avoid a failure only after the environment has produced one.
Learned world models offer another route, because most policy updates can be taken against imagined rollouts instead of collected ones.
We propose \emph{Cost-Aware MAMBA} (CA-MAMBA), which augments a multi-agent recurrent state-space model with per-agent cost prediction, trains factorized actors on imagined reward and cost advantages, and calibrates one team-level Lagrange multiplier from measured environment cost.
We characterize when this transfers: bounded one-step cost and transition error imply an explicit finite-horizon bound on the gap between imagined and real cost, which fixes the conservative margin an imagined constraint must carry.
Empirically, at a matched interaction budget CA-MAMBA lowers casualty cost on all 24 shared StarCraft configurations and wins more often on all 24, while the model-free baseline wins no episode on 22 of them; the ordering largely persists when that baseline is given fifty times more data.
On the SafePO velocity benchmark, at a matched budget it holds mean cost under the limit while earning 2.8 to 12 times the reward of both MACPO and MAPPO-Lagrangian in all three environments.

% ===================== BACKGROUND =====================
\section{Scientific Background}

\subsection{Constrained Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning}

In cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), a team of agents must learn decentralized policies that maximize a shared reward signal under partial observability.
Adding safety constraints turns this into a \emph{constrained} Dec-POMDP~[1]: the team must keep an expected cumulative cost below a given budget $d$ while maximizing return.

Constrained policy optimization~[2] controls expected cost through trust-region updates.
MACPO and MAPPO-Lagrangian carry this line into multi-agent robot control~[7], both built on MAPPO~[8].
Related formulations include CMIX, which adds peak to average constraints in a value-factorized learner~[9], and risk-sensitive methods such as RMIX~[10].
Every update in this family is computed from environment trajectories---exactly the cost the method is trying to avoid paying.

\subsection{Model-Based Reinforcement Learning and World Models}

Dreamer~[3] improves a policy through rollouts of learned recurrent latent dynamics, a line since scaled across domains~[4].
SafeDreamer adds cost prediction, separate reward and cost critics, and Lagrangian optimization to this single-agent framework~[6].
MAMBA adapts latent world models to cooperative partial observability by aggregating other agents' latents and actions through attention~[5].

None of these three systems, by itself, addresses the multi-agent constrained setting: Dreamer and SafeDreamer are single-agent, and MAMBA has no cost channel.
The composition of these components---and the questions it raises about cost aggregation, communication, and model error in the constraint---is the gap this research addresses.

% ===================== RESEARCH GOALS =====================
\section{Research Goals}

The research aims to answer the following questions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Cost-aware world model for MARL.} Can a multi-agent world model be extended with per-agent cost prediction so that a factorized policy trained on imagined rollouts achieves lower realized safety cost than model-free constrained baselines at a matched interaction budget?

    \item \textbf{Theoretical transfer guarantees.} Under what conditions does a constraint enforced in imagination transfer to the real environment? Specifically, can bounded one-step cost-head error and transition error yield an explicit finite-horizon bound on the gap between imagined and realized cost?

    \item \textbf{Communication requirements.} How much inter-agent communication is necessary for the constrained policy to function? Is this a property of the domain rather than of the framework, and can it be measured by varying the attention neighborhood at execution time?

    \item \textbf{Generalization across cost structures.} Does the approach generalize across different cost signals (sparse/discrete casualty cost on SMAC vs.\ continuous velocity constraints on MAMuJoCo) and action spaces (discrete vs.\ continuous)?
\end{enumerate}

% ===================== METHODOLOGY =====================
\section{Research Methodology}

\subsection{Constrained Dec-POMDP Formulation}

We formulate the problem as a finite-horizon constrained Dec-POMDP $\mathcal{M} = \langle \mathcal{I}, \mathcal{S}, \{\mathcal{A}_i\}, P, R, \{C_i\}, \{\mathcal{O}_i\}, \Omega, \mu_0, H, \gamma, d \rangle$, where each agent~$i$ has a local cost $C_i$ and the team cost is the mean $c_t = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i} c_t^i$.
The joint policy factorizes as $\pi_\phi(\mathbf{a}_t \mid \boldsymbol{\tau}_t, \mathbf{m}_t) = \prod_{i=1}^{N} \pi_{\phi_i}^i(a_t^i \mid \tau_t^i, m_t^i)$, where $m_t^i$ is a message from attended teammates.
The objective is to maximize discounted return $J_R$ subject to $J_C(\pi_\phi) \leq d$.

\subsection{Cost-Aware Multi-Agent World Model (CA-MAMBA)}

The world model is a recurrent state-space model (RSSM) with per-agent deterministic state $h_t^i$ and stochastic state $z_t^i$.
Three heads decode each latent state: observation (for representation learning), reward, and \emph{cost}---the latter is our addition.
Each cost head regresses agent~$i$'s own cost $c_t^i$; the imagined team cost is the mean $\hat{c}_t = \frac{1}{N}\sum_i \hat{c}_t^i$.

Inter-agent communication is realized via a multi-head Transformer encoder that aggregates teammates' previous actions and stochastic latents, parameterized by an attention neighborhood $\mathcal{N}_i$.
Setting $\mathcal{N}_i = \mathcal{I}$ gives all-to-all communication; $\mathcal{N}_i = \{i\}$ yields communication-free execution.

\subsection{Imagined Actor--Critic and Lagrangian Dual}

A Lagrange multiplier $\beta \geq 0$ relaxes the constraint.
Two separate critics estimate imagined reward and cost returns, yielding a combined advantage $A_u^\beta = A_u^R - \beta A_u^C$.
The actor maximizes a clipped surrogate objective over imagined samples.
The multiplier is updated from \emph{measured} environment cost: $\beta_{k+1} = [\beta_k + \alpha_\beta(\bar{C}_k - d)]_+$, creating a hybrid where the actor uses imagined cost but the dual uses real cost.

\subsection{Theoretical Analysis}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Cost Transfer]
Under bounded one-step cost error $\epsilon_c$ and transition error $\epsilon_P$ (in total variation), the gap between imagined and real discounted cost over horizon $H_{\mathrm{im}}$ satisfies:
\[
|J_{C,H_{\mathrm{im}}}(\pi) - \widehat{J}_{C,H_{\mathrm{im}}}(\pi)| \leq \epsilon_c \sum_{t=0}^{H_{\mathrm{im}}-1} \gamma^t + c_{\max} \epsilon_P \sum_{t=0}^{H_{\mathrm{im}}-1} t\gamma^t.
\]
\end{theorem}

This bound identifies cost-head error as entering linearly in the horizon and transition error quadratically, naming which error source to reduce first.
We further show that the measured-cost dual update has the correct sign (it is projected stochastic descent in $\beta$) but cannot repair an actor gradient taken through a cost-blind model.

\subsection{Experimental Plan}

\textbf{Environments:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \emph{StarCraft Multi-Agent Challenge (SMAC):} 12 maps with casualty cost (sparse, discrete, irreversible) and collision cost (dense, continuous, reversible).
    \item \emph{MAMuJoCo (SafePO):} Three velocity-constrained locomotion tasks with continuous actions (Ant~$2{\times}4$, Ant~$4{\times}2$, HalfCheetah~$2{\times}3$).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Baselines:} MACPO-Discrete (SMAC), MACPO and MAPPO-Lagrangian (MAMuJoCo), all from the SafePO repository.

\textbf{Ablations:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item Communication: full ($\mathcal{N}_i = \mathcal{I}$) vs.\ self-only ($\mathcal{N}_i = \{i\}$) at execution time.
    \item Multiplier learning rate $\alpha_\beta$: effect on cost--reward trade-off.
    \item Cost machinery removal: unconstrained world-model learner to isolate the cost mechanism's contribution.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Metrics:} Win rate and shaped score (SMAC); episodic reward and cost (MAMuJoCo); cost-head one-step prediction error; multiplier dynamics.

\subsection{Preliminary Results}

Preliminary experiments confirm the approach.
At a matched budget of 100,000 interactions, CA-MAMBA has lower mean casualty cost than MACPO-Discrete in 24/24 SMAC configurations and higher win rate in 24/24; the baseline wins zero episodes on 22 of 24 configurations.
On MAMuJoCo at a matched 1M budget, CA-MAMBA earns 2.8--12$\times$ the reward of both baselines in all three environments while holding cost under the limit.
The cost head's measured one-step error is at most 0.004 (${\sim}3\%$ of peak cost), making the transfer bound quantitative.
